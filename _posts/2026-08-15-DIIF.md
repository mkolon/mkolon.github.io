### Monday August 24

- Banks use security equipment from many companies. One of the most common of these is [**Fortinet**](https://www.fortinet.com). The [**credentials used to access and configure**](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/06/17/massive-password-stealing-attack-hits-75k-fortinet-firewalls/5257877) these firewalls are rather important! This is what Sean mentioned is widely being called the "Fortibleed" exploit. To their credit, Fortinet is [**being pretty transparent**](https://www.fortinet.com/blog/psirt-blogs/analysis-of-reported-credential-compromise-of-fortigate-devices) about what happened and what they're doing to try to, uh, stop the bleeding. 


- As we will see, firewalls and other security elements are themselves [**continually under attack**](https://www.databreachtoday.com/cisco-fixes-firewall-0-days-after-likely-nation-state-hack-a-24934). This is just one recent example that could compromise community banks given the type of device targeted. 

- Two more banks [**notifying thousands of victims**](https://therecord.media/banks-marquis-software-ransomware) about Marquis Software ~~ransomware~~ attack. This exploit involved an attack on Marquis' Sonicwall firewall.

- State-backed hackers have been [**quietly exploiting a fresh zero-day in Palo Alto Networks firewalls**](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/05/07/state-backed-hackers-hammer-palo-alto-firewall-zero-day-before-patch-lands/5234737) to gain root access with no login required.
